    Dedication

    This article is dedicated to the 12 Police Officers from around the world that I met in my lifetime that are members of the Baha’i Faith. Among them, I mention 2. My brother-in-arms, Kelly Jenkins, who is the only person in this life who has actively shared my three areas of Police, Baha’i Faith, and Gaming. And Mr. Dale Hogue, who was the first and only Baha’i that was also an F.B.I. Agent and the Head of Security at the big Baha’i Conferences. Mr. Hogue is an inspiration because, apart from his integrity, he was always a very humble guy in spite of his position.
    Apart from these 2 mentioned, there is actually a Baha’i International Association for Law Enforcement – or whatever their official name is nowadays, where I met the others.
    I believe that to a large extent, this article is dedicated to the future, when there will be more Baha’is that are police officers.

    For Baha’i Police Officers

    1) Intro

    This topic was originally the subject intended for an entire book, and I need to be very careful about this article becoming too big – especially as there are hundreds of Writings on the topic of Justice and Society in the Baha’i Faith. It does not matter if you were born and raised in a Baha’i family and became a Police Officer, or if you were a Police Officer that came across the Baha’i Faith and decided to join it, this article applies to you just the same. Also, I need to point out that many of the claims that I lay in this article may be contrary to what most religious people believe – people of all religions – so I have to be extra careful to list my sources.

    2) A minority

    Being both a member of the Baha’i Faith, and of the Police Force, you are a member of two minorities in this world, that are both very misunderstood by mankind. The second even more misunderstood than the first one. So you’re a minority within a minority – however you add that up. There are about 8 and half million Baha’is out of a little less than 8 billion in the world. as of the writing of this article. There are more Police Officers but it’s still quite a minority. Now guess what? When you put the two together, in my entire lifetime, I have only known of close to a dozen people that were Baha’is and Police Officers. And that’s 12 out of 8 billion people.

    3) The first Baha’i cops

    The first question that comes to mind is: Who were the first Baha’i cops? Well, that depends on who exactly you consider to be Police Officers. Do jailers count as cops? Or does being a Policeman count once you start with Patrol? Most Agencies today do not consider jailers as full Police Officers. But in any case, let us answer both.

    When they exiled the Bab to the Fortress of Mahku, on the outskirts of the Persian empire, his jailers went from being told that he was an enemy of the State all the way to adoring him, with many having converted to His Faith. There is ample evidence that they would have even let Him walk free – if He Himself had ever harbored such an intention. Alarmed by this situation, the government of the Persian Empire eventually exiled him further to the outpost and prison of Chiriq. And yet the story repeated itself, again. Even the villagers of Mahku and Chiriq loved the prisoner and asked of the Bab a blessing for their day, and often asked the Bab to settle matters of Justice and settle disputes – from His prison cell! The jailers of the prisons of Mahku and Chiriq are thus the first Baha’i jailers in history.

    As for what most people would consider Police Officers, when they had the final trial against the Bab to decide His execution, the fanatic Islamic religious leaders were under the belief that he was poisoning only the minds of Muslims, so they hired an entire Christian division of mounted cavalry of about 2000 to escort the prisoner to Tabriz for his final trial. This division was Armenian. Well, by the time they arrived at Tabriz, the 2000 mounted division had each and all converted to the Faith of the Bab – including their commander, a Lieutenant – and what had started as the escort of a prisoner became the escort of their personal Lord. Alarmed by this situation, the fanatic religious leaders against the Bab ordered the cavalry not to enter the city gates.

    Well that Christian mounted cavalry that became Babis can be considered the first Baha’i cops, as they are comparable to a division of State Police (America) or Gendarmerie (France).

    [I recommend reading passages about the Bab in the “Dawn Breakers” by Nabil and “Prescription for Living” by Ruyyih Rabbani (Khanum) for more details of the events described above.]

    While I am on the topic of the Bab’s final trial, I have to say a few things. Most of Baha’i history was witnessed by people of other religions – mainly Muslims and Christians – as well as Baha’is. So from that point of view, there is a lot of legitimacy to the events documented by Baha’i historians. However, there is a huge point of disagreement, and that is the Bab’s final trial ordering His execution. Baha’is and Muslims from Persia disagree about the nature of this trial.

    The greatest scholar of the Baha’i Faith, Mirza Abu’l-Fadl, has explained in his book called “The Baha’i Proofs” that any neutral observer will see that the intention of the Bab’s final trial was never to establish whether there was any truth to the message that He brought, but instead to brandish Him a troublemaker and set His execution.
    [Which alas, is a tactic way too familiar to anyone studying their own religious history. – Ryan]

    To emphasize this point, the fanatical religious leaders had first called the top General of the Persian Army and asked him to carry out the execution. This General responded to them, "If you ask me to wage war against any other Nation on earth, for the glory of Persia, I would gladly do it. But today, you ask me to kill a man so pious that He never violated a single of the 5 pillars of Islam. I will not be a part of your MURDER."
    [Not exact quote, except for the last sentence.]

    [NOTE: The religion of Islam has five pillars:
    Prayer
    Fasting,
    The giving to the poor
    Pilgrimage
    Declaration of Faith.]

    4) Writings (Scripture) about Police Officers

    The next question is: Are there any specific Writings (Scripture) about Police Officers?

    According to my mentor growing up, the late Doctor Sadeghzadeh-Milani, [may he Rest in Peace (R.I.P.)] who was a “Knight of Baha’u’llah” and twice in his life an Auxiliary Board Member – first under the Hands of the Cause and later under the Counsellors - the answer is YES. However, those Writings are still part of the ocean of Writings in Persian that have not yet been translated into another language.

    Supposedly, there are two passages.

    The first passage states that in the future Society of Mankind, Police Officers will be chosen by taking those children who are so truthful that they would prefer to have their throats cut off rather than tell a lie, and those kids once adults would be cops. In other words, it would have to be people whose testimonies not a single member of Society would ever doubt.

    Now it is obvious that, as of the writing of this article, such beings do not exist yet. (I have seen Baha’i children who refused to lie – I myself was one of them – but never to the extent of preferring to have their throats cut off than to lie.) But it is my belief that generation after generation raised in the Faith will eventually produce such beings.

    The brilliant Adib Taherzadeh explains in his masterpiece “Revelation of Baha’u’llah” how once Baha’u’llah puts something in writing, the creative energies of the Word of God actually create such beings as described. Among many passages on this theme, I refer you to his fantastic chapter in Volume 3 on the very popular prayer, “The Tablet of Ahmad”.

    We now come to the second passage, which I know many Baha’is would want to see for themselves and will totally doubt truly exists. And the second passage that my mentor explained to me was that Police Officers would be allowed to lie in order to obtain the truth. Anybody familiar with Police work, a.k.a. finding out that criminal activity is taking place, is very familiar with this principle.
    Now in the United States of America, there is a similar principle in law. A Police Officer is allowed to lie on the condition that an innocent person would know it is a lie – and the guilty one would be the one who does not know.
    As a very simple example, suppose someone shoplifted from a store. If you tell the person “We know you were in the store”, an innocent person would not know that it is a lie. So that is not acceptable – legally speaking. However, if you state, “You know that the place is full of cameras. Let’s go back for a positive identification!” then an innocent person would know clearly that you are lying – but the actual shoplifter would not.

    5) Discussion on religious forgiveness versus religious laws of protection of society.

    In general, it is my opinion that Christ left many Writings on personal salvation and forgiveness, but that His teachings are seriously lacking on the whole aspect of regulating Society as a whole. And the contrary applies to Muhammad who left many laws for Society’s right to protect itself and what punishments apply to what crime, but did not emphasize enough individual forgiveness. [Granted here that they had different audiences… And that my personal opinion is that Christ having only 3 years to live, well, he was seriously OUT OF TIME.]

    Using the Math letters x, y, z, as an example, the Baha’i Faith explains the whole religious situation as follows: x slaps y. y forgives x. But z, another element, Society… Has to come and ask x “Why did you slap y?”. This is where, of course, Police Officers come into play in the Field. And Spiritual Assemblies (the future Local Houses of Justice) come into play in a meeting (court) setting.

    While I am on the topic of religious law, I need to bring out what I’ve witnessed and observed during my lifetime. The civil law, how to get married and change your name and contracts, etc., is way different from place to place, with thousands of ways of doing things.
    However, where the criminal law is concerned, although it is very different around the world, there has been a worldwide desire to converge and match the criminal law and make international agreements. So in my lifetime, I have noticed that the world’s criminal laws have converged towards the Baha’i Law as stipulated in the Kitab-i-Aqdas (book of Laws).

    6) The dangers of Religion

    There are some very dangerous things that can occur in religious communities, because people take the principle of individual forgiveness too far – and do not protect their people.

    The first thing you will find is what is called in Criminal Justice Conflict Theory. This is when someone has been in an abusive situation for a long time, maybe even their whole life (born in it), and the rest of Society tells them their situation is abusive… But the victim knows only this situation and tells Society that everything is fine. This is why this theory is labelled a conflict. The conflict occurs between the victim and Society.

    The other thing that you can find is revictimization. And that is when you tell the victim of a crime to forgive their aggressor. You make them a victim again (twice) by doing this. The worst thing you can ever do a person is betray them. And there is no betrayal on this earth greater than offering a victim of crime to their aggressor.
    The principle of individual forgiveness does not apply to others. Forgiveness can not be chosen for another – especially a child.

    I personally know a lady who was raped as a child, and whose family told her that because she was a Baha’i, she had to forgive the man – and even be around him at social events. Well, this child – this little girl - told God: “God, if this is the Baha’i Faith, then I want nothing to do with it. Show me!” and she opened a Baha’i book to a completely random page. Her eyes fell on a passage from Abdu’l-Baha that explained that you have to protect the sheep from the wolf, and that you do not simply offer the sheep to the wolf…

    Let me explain things in a very different way. The Baha’i Faith, and even Christianity for that matter, were not created for the purpose of making people victims of crime.

    7) Self Defense

    This leads us to the whole discussion on self-defense and on defense of others.

    There is a very big problem in the Baha’i Faith. And that problem is that the quasi-majority of the Baha’i population, having learned that you can not fight back in matters of Religion – Religious fighting being abolished, even in self-defense – all of these Baha’i friends have come to believe that they can not defend themselves from criminals.

    This is completely and utterly ridiculous to me. But you will see this everywhere in the current state of the Baha’i community.

    So let’s jump straight into the matter. The old compilation called “Lights of Guidance” that had hundreds of letters from the Guardian (Shoghi Effendi) and from the Universal House of Justice, organized by topic, had a section on self-defense. In one passage, the Guardian authorized an isolated pioneer to have a firearm in his house - specifically to protect his family from bandits, because they lived too far from a timely Police response.

    Shoghi Effendi also stated elsewhere that Baha’i children had a full right to fight back bullies at schools, and did not have to bow down to bullies simply because they were Baha’is.

    Baha’u’llah himself encouraged the learning of martial arts. [And I have to point out here that martial arts are not merely about defending yourself, the very first lesson to master is that of self-control.]

    In general, a Baha’i should not carry a firearm unless he or she feels it is necessary. Though we are talking about Police Officers in this article – for which a firearm is even mandatory – there are situations when others need one. For example, we had a businessman in my native Country who was making a good deal of money and whose family had been attacked by armed men at his very house on 4 different occasions. He felt it was necessary to carry a firearm (and YES, he was a Baha’i), and the government of that Country who was known for being strict on firearm licenses issued him a license to carry a gun.

    So basically, when a Baha’i tells you Baha’u’llah’s injunction that “It is better to be killed than to kill”, that Baha’i himself or herself does not even know that statement was made in matters of Religion, meaning that such a thing as a Holy War is completely and utterly forbidden. But that statement does not apply to someone defending themselves or their families from criminals.

    I have to say here that sometimes the issue of why a person was killed - exactly - becomes extremely blurry. I was raised in Africa, and of the 27 Hands of the Cause that were alive when Shoghi Effendi died, one of them was an African believer by the name of Enoch Olinga. His deep knowledge of the Writings of the Faith and his many years of experience coupled together to make him a true shield for the Baha’i Community when the civil war erupted in the Country of Uganda – where we have the first Baha’i Temple of the African Continent.

    The death of Enoch Olinga raises a question as to whether he was killed as part of an act of crime, or specifically for his high role in the Baha’i Faith. [Or even both, why not? – Ryan]
    Some armed men entered his home during the civil war, and robbed his house, and were very quick to shoot him down. To this day, the Baha’i community is very unclear about the ultimate reason behind this murder.

    8) Defense of others

    Many Countries in the world have a law in place for “Defense of others”, meaning that self-defense can transfer to someone else. This has to be the case to allow everyday citizens to protect the elderly, children, women, etc. If a person is attacked, another person can defend them with the same level of Force that they themselves would be allowed. So an old person attacked can be helped out by a young person that is nearby.

    I have come to believe that in matters of Faith, you have to forgive what is done to you, but you cannot forgive what is done to others, and you have to stand up for others. That is both your Religious and your civic duty. Now I am very sure at this point that many Baha’is and Christians and Muslims even would disagree with me. But this is my overall understanding of the Holy Writings of all Faiths that I have explored. (And YES, I have limited knowledge as compared to many people I know.)

    In my native Country of Ivory Coast, Africa, my favorite law is in the Penal Code (the Criminal Code), article 107. It is called “Failure to render assistance.” This law states that if you see someone in trouble and you fail to help them out, you yourself go to jail for about half a year. In the event that there is a danger to yourself in helping them out, you are expected to help however you can – even if all you can do is call the Police or Firemen, etc. Now in the event that you are part of what my Country’s laws calls Emergency Services, meaning Police, Fire, Ambulance, Doctors and Nurses, etc., and you fail to render assistance, then the punishment is doubled to an entire year!
    [And believe me, you do NOT want to spend a year in an African prison!]

    So in my Country, if you fail to help a person that is in any type of trouble, you have committed the crime of utterly failing your moral obligation to help your fellow citizens.

    9) Importance of compassion

    One of the Attributes of God that all of the world’s major Religions teaches is Compassion – which we ourselves are meant to develop. You may ask yourself why I am picking one Attribute out of a hundred for a discussion that has to do with Police Officers that are Baha’is. So let me start by saying that compassion has everything to do with it!

    When I started my first degree in the 90’s, there was a study released by the Psychology Department that compared sociopaths to police and fire heroes. This study determined that the two groups were exactly the same in every single aspect – except for one thing. As some examples, both groups were the best with weapons and tools, both groups got off on thrills and excitements of very dangerous situations, both groups eventually became desensitized to their environments, etc.
    The only difference between the two groups was that police and fire heroes never lost compassion for people who are suffering. No matter how desensitized to their environments they became, they still had compassion for those who suffer. That was the little-bitty difference between the good guys and the bad guys.

    Another thing about compassion. Serial Killers, the best killers of this world who kill many people and outsmart the police, have three traits as children: Bed wetting to a late age, obsession with fire, and cruelty to animals.
    In the category of compassion falls the fact that every single Religion on earth teaches respect for Nature and kindness to animals.

    This is, by the way, in my opinion, the most important of the three traits serial killers have as children: cruelty to animals. I say this because I myself had the other two traits of bed wetting to a later age, and unusual fascination with studying fire. But I never, ever, had any cruelty to animals. Quite the contrary, my sister and I had pet cats, dogs, rabbits, an owl, even a hawk at one point!

    I did not end up a serial killer but when I was a Policeman, I found out that I myself was a possible suspect of being a serial killer on at least one occasion. Because of that fact, I could no longer come forward with the things I had discovered myself. First, the victims were women of different colors of races, and I was the only one who figured out they had the exact same type of eyebrows. Second, the killer took souvenirs from the victims, such as their purses or necklaces. I figured out from there and the timing of the killings that it was the same serial killer they were looking for in Texas – where similar killings took place when none took place in Louisiana (where I was). And third of all, I personally spoke to women who got away from the killer, but whose description was different from the guy they were looking for – the drawing they were passing out. (The description sort of matched ME, but the women knew it was not ME.)
    Since I myself was a suspect, if I had come out with any of that information, it would have been used to say I was the killer myself.

    But the point made here is that cruelty to animals is a very deadly trait in a child, and love of animals should be cultivated.

    10) Principle of Loyalty to the Government

    I held a discussion on this in my article on “Different Levels of law”.

    There are many Principles in the Baha’i Faith and when conflicts occur, some supersede others. The Principle of Loyalty to the Government is extremely important in the Baha’i Faith, and often supersedes.

    When our greatest scholar Mirza Abdu’l-Fadl was summoned to meet the Prime Minister of his time, the Vizir, he told this Vizir: “Any government in this world would be down on their knees BEGGING to have citizens that are completely honest and so loyal that even after you kill their members, they are still loyal to their government.”
    And the Prime Minister told him, “You are absolutely RIGHT!”

    Hand of the Cause William Sears, the American T.V. and sports personality (journalist), stated in his book “God loves laughter” that Baha’is are pre-disposed for government work, because the Faith teaches them to be loyal to the government, they are honest, and they are forbidden from getting involved in partisan politics.
    [Meaning, they will never run for office against their boss. - Ryan]

    The Principle of Loyalty to the Government is so powerful that even if a government forbids Baha’i gatherings, we cancel our regular meetings and prayer sessions, and we only meet in groups of 2 or 3.

    However, I have to say here that every Principle has its limits. And the Principle of Loyalty to the Government does NOT apply when your personal salvation is at stake. For example, if the government tells you to stop praying altogether or forbids you from doing your month of fasting, then you cannot obey, because your personal salvation would be at stake.

    In all other matters, however, Baha’is have to obey their government.

    11) Forgiveness does NOT apply to stealing

    All Religions pretty much teach NOT to forgive the thief.

    In the Old Testament, in the Bible, for example, it equates the act of stealing to that of MURDER. It is extremely strong language. It says the thief comes to kill you.

    Likewise, in the Baha’i Faith, Abdu'l-Baha teaches that you cannot forgive either the thief or the tyrant, because if you forgive either one, all you are doing is encouraging them to keep doing what they are doing.
