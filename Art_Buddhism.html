Some Thoughts About Buddhism

    (Written by a Baha’i)

    Buddha, just like Baha’u’llah, was born into nobility. But they both gave up that life in order to have a greater spiritual purpose. Both of them withdrew from society for a number of years before coming out and announcing their message. Baha’u’llah went to the mountains, and Buddha to the forest.

    There was also a poisoning incident in both their lives. Baha’u’llah was poisoned by his half- brother. The poison was supposed to kill him. He survived, but it destroyed his exquisite handwriting and hurt his health. In the case of Buddha, his cousin was going to poison him to death, but the cousin had a small cut on his finger, and the poison got to him and killed him, before he could poison Buddha.

    Buddhists believe that Buddha was born of immaculate conception (virgin mother), just like Christians believe Christ was as well. Except Buddha came 5,000 years before Christ.

    Buddha was the first to teach that the pursuit of happiness in this life is the pursuit and development of virtues, becoming a better person.

    Buddha also taught to focus on today and the present, not to live in the past or in the future. His words regarding this are:

    "Yesterday is but a dream
    And tomorrow is only a vision
    But today well lived
    Makes every yesterday a dream of happiness
    And every tomorrow a vision of hope
    Look well therefore to this day. "

    Buddha talked about 3 types of friends that you have in life. He uses this to illustrate something. He said that if you decide to go on a trip to another land, you have 3 types of friends. The first friend tells you goodbye at the house and lets you go. The second friend goes with you all the way to the station, and sees you off on your trip, wishing you well. But the third friend is so close to you that he says he will go with you to the other land, and travel with you all the way.

    Buddha explains that is what happens when you die. The first friend that does not leave the house, and tells you goodbye there, is your money and material possessions. The second friend that goes with you to the station to see you off is your family and friends, who go to your funeral and see you off on your journey to the afterlife. But the third friend that goes all the way with you is your soul.

    Next comes the whole concept of heaven and hell. Every Founder of the world’s great Religions described hell as an unpleasant place, and heaven as a good place. Christ and Muhammad, who came from the hot middle east, described hell as a very hot place. Yet Zoroaster, who came from cold snowy mountains, described hell a very cold place, and heaven as a warm fire. With the coming of the Baha’i Faith, heaven and hell become symbolic and are considered metaphors with many explanations. However, I still prefer Buddha’s explanation of heaven and hell the best.

    Buddha said that heaven and hell are two tables at which you can find the best food dishes from all over the world. The only bad thing is that you can not bend your elbows. Your arms have to stay straight. At the table called hell, everyone is out to feed themselves, and they have trouble getting any food. But at the table called heaven, everyone is making sure that the person to their left and their right are eating. So at the table called heaven, everyone gets to eat.

    The next thing is that the lotus flower is considered holy in all religions. But this started with Buddhism first. The lotus flower raises itself above the mud that it comes from, and then opens itself up to the sun. It follows the sun all day, and when the sun sets, the flower closes up. But it still stays above the mud. This is symbolic of our spiritual journey. We have to raise ourselves above the mud that we come from, and then open ourselves up to God and follow him.

    Note that the Baha’i temple in India is in the shape of a lotus flower, and everyone loves it in that country.

    Buddhists do not believe in a humanoid God, such as an old man with a grey beard. But they do believe in an essence of essences or a beginning of beginnings.

    Also, Zhen Buddhist meditation is used in martial arts. It teaches you to look beyond pain, and to empty your mind, which helps in combat.

    Also, regarding reincarnation, people believed in that way before Buddhism. So they added that concept to Buddhism. In fact, if you study the writings of Buddha, he does talk about a type of reincarnation. But it is not reincarnation for people like you and me. Buddha speaks of Messianic Reincarnation, which is the return of the Messiah from age to age, the Holy Spirit.

    The last thing I will mention about Buddhism is that Buddha’s 12 disciples brought 12,000 people to Buddhism in one day. In Christianity, something similar happened when apostle Peter brought 1,000 to Christianity 3 days after Christ was crucified.

    Well, as the story goes, before Buddha let loose his 12 disciples, he put them to the test.

    Buddha asked, " Where will you go? And what will you do? "
    The disciples replied, " We will go everywhere and we will enlighten the people. "
    Buddha then asked, " And if they refuse to hear your message? "
    They replied, " Then we will be happy, for they do not wish to harm us. "
    Buddha, " And what if they beat you and put you in jail? "
    They replied, " Then we will be happy, for they do not wish to kill us. "
    Buddha, " And if they kill you for the message you bring? "
    They replied, " Then we will be happy to die for the glory of God, "
    After a moment of silence, Buddha concluded, " Well done! "

      Keny Ramzai
      May 2020
