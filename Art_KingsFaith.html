On (Just) Kings and Faith

    I recently watched an international hit, a four hour movie called, “The Last Emperor”, the story of the last king of China who inherited his throne at the age of three, lost it in Chinese Revolution, rebecame emperor of Manchuria, and was captured by Communists in World War II, and released after ten years of prison.

    It was fantastic! I loved it from the start. The timing between past and future was perfect. Also, I was impressed at how the little kids played their roles so well, specifically the three-year old emperor and the seven-year old one. It was cool to see that to teach the kid emperor the layout of the Forbidden City, they made a wooden model of all the buildings.

    Baha’u’llah predicted that as the world matures, the world would move away from having kings. However, He predicted that way later, when the world matures even more, humanity would go back to having kings.

    Baha’u’llah explains that a just king is the most efficient form of government, and that a king that is truly just is worth dying for. Kingship also represents God’s rule.

    Shoghi Effendi, the Guardian of the Baha’i Faith, wrote in different styles to East and to West. To the West, he wrote with logical explanations and the reasons for things. For that is how the people in the West like it. To the East, however, he wrote as a king to his vassals. For people in the East like having a king.

    In history, the best two kings to ever exist were father and son (in my opinion). That would be king David, the embodiment of justice, and king Solomon, the embodiment of wisdom. The father wrote Psalms and the son wrote Proverbs, in the Torah (the Old Testament). They united their known world, and got rid of God’s enemies. It took two generations to do it, but they created a kingdom like no other. Under David and Solomon, you could buy anything in the kingdom for the original price, no matter where it came from. This included pure bred Arab horses, the best in the world, and the cedar trees from Lebanon, which was the best wood of the time to build houses or temples. The people were truly happy. No one went hungry. There were temples of justice and temples to worship God. The government also had plenty of money. Basically, David and Solomon created the Kingdom of Heaven on earth – for the part of the world that they knew about.

    Also, God promised Abraham he would be the father of kings. Abraham thus became the father of rulers, meaning physical kings. But more importantly, he became the father of Spiritual Kings, the Founders of world religions.

    Abraham had three wives. Moses and Jesus are descendants of Abraham through Sarah, his first wife. Muhammad and the Bab are descendants of Abraham through Hagar, his second wife. Baha’u’llah is descendant of Abraham through Katurah, his third wife. So finally, Abraham is father to five world religions, the Jewish Faith, the Christian Faith, the Muslim Faith, the Babi Faith and the Baha’i Faith. Pretty cool, huh?

    The only king of Baha’u’llah’s time that did not go against Him was queen Victoria of England. She said, “If this thing is from God, nothing will stop it from progressing. But if it’s not from God, it can do no harm.” Of all those kings, only Victoria’s lineage is still alive today, and involved in ruling England.

    The big irony, where kings are concerned in Baha’u’llah’s life, is the reversal of position. Baha’u’llah was made a prisoner, exiled, tortured, beaten, poisoned, with threat of death hanging over Him almost 50 years, with never a single charge filed against Him in any court of law. At the beginning, the world was ruled by kings who were power hungry and greedy and took their positions for granted. Baha’u’llah warned them to change their ways and advised them of things to do to improve their kingdoms for their people. He even told them exactly what would happen to them if they didn’t change. They all went against Him, except for queen Victoria.

    By the end of Baha’u’llah’s earthly life, all those kings lost their thrones, and the world got rid of kings (except Victoria). And at that time, Baha’u’llah was a King that rulers would envy, with followers in 15 countries and from every religious background. So the situation reversed.

    Some rulers became Baha’i throughout history.

    The queen of Romania became Baha’i at the time of Shoghi Effendi. She was the first ruler in history to do so. She was taught the Faith by Martha Root, an American Baha’i that traveled the entire world several times and taught the Faith wherever adventures took her. Martha Root also left a great diary behind for posterity.

    When I was younger, the king of the Solomon Islands was also a Baha’i. And he would go to the Baha’i World Center (BWC) in Israel to get advice from the top of the Faith. Note that at that time, the Solomon Islands hosted one of the seven Baha’i temples in the world.

    There have been kings in history who conquered lands and incorporated them, uniting huge empires, such as Alexander the Great and Cyrus of Persia.

    I believe the greatest Muslim king ever was by far Saladin. The man was no good with a sword or a bow. But he was a master of both strategy and diplomacy. Saladin united 40 warring factions and tribes of his time. It was both he and Richard the Lionheart, on the Christian side, who ended the second crusade in the Holy Land. Both men had developed true respect for each other. And they knew no one could control both Jerusalem and the supply route from the ocean. Each faction could only hold one or the other. So Saladin and Richard the Lionheart created a united Jerusalem, where both Christians and Muslims were allowed. It is interesting to note that during the fighting, when Saladin took Jerusalem, he was seen a liberator instead of another conqueror, because Saladin was the only one who made sure that not a single church got damaged, no matter which religion it belonged to.

    I now come to the most famous legend around the world, that of king Arthur and Merlin. This legend has existed for ages, thousands of years. However, in writing, the legend is 1500 years old. There are over 2000 unique books about the legend of Arthur and his knights. One library in England has collected all of them. And they have a specialist that read them all. There are people all over Great Britain who believe that their country side or village or town are related to places associated with the legend. And the place where Merlin is from is still fighting for its independence to this very day.

    The reason Arthur stories differ from one another is that some things we know, and some things we don’t. For example, we know that both Arthur and Mordred died at the Battle of Camlann which is a pass in between two high areas. But we do not know if Arthur and Mordred died separately, or if they killed each other as the generals of the two armies.

    Regardless, the legend of the just king Arthur, who united all 5 warring kingdoms of Albion, is a legend that has inspired many, including U.S. president Kennedy.

    The French also have a legend of an early just king. And that story also has an old wise wizard and noble knights. Though it takes place in a different time period, it is a very similar legend.

    My favorite Arthur and Merlin version is a relatively recent British series called “The Adventures of Merlin”. It lasted 5 seasons, say 65 episodes (13 per season). It takes Merlin, Arthur, Morgana, Guineviere, and the knights Lancelot, Gwaine, Percival, Galahad, etc… when they were all young and developing, and first getting to know each other. Arthur is not king yet, and Merlin is still learning his magic. The series takes you all the way to the actual legend. I mention this series because of the unexpected success it had. They were hoping it would be popular in England, where it aired at prime time. Also, 3 channels in America aired it, including PBS and BBC America. But the first year alone, the show became a top hit in 180 countries, and was translated all over the world. The entire globe loves the legend of Arthur and Merlin.

    The next thing worth mentioning is the rules for kings in Africa, the oldest land. The African system was set up as “zero tolerance for mistakes”. I will give two examples.

    First, in Africa, the successor to the king was not his son. We, the population, have no way of knowing if the king’s wife cheated on him or not. So the one to rule after a king was his sister’s son, his nephew. No matter who the father is, the mother is the sister of the king, the royal blood. Her son has to be 100% royal blood.

    Next is something the rest of the world considers barbaric. That is… When the king dies, his entire court dies with him, including anyone who worked for him.

    In this fashion, there was no internal poisoning or assassination, only absolute loyalty. Not only that, but everyone wants the king both happy and healthy. Because when he dies, you die with him.

    A very famous king in Africa was Chaka Zulu in South Africa. Tribes used to fight lined up in straight lines. Chaka was a strategist on the battlefield and changed his units’ attack positions. He overtook and incorporated all the other tribes.

    Haley Selassi of Ethiopia is also very famous for having lions as bodyguards, fighting off Italian colonists (and kicking them out), and making a speech about racial equality and war that Bob Marley turned into a song.
