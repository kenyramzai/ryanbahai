Comments on the Tablet of Ahmad
(Most popular Baha'i prayer)

    Introduction

    I have written a total of 3 articles on the Tablet of Ahmad in the last 8 years. Unfortunately, I do not have possession of them, at this time. Hopefully, I can backtrack them and get them from friends who received those articles. In the meantime, during the last week, this has been a topic of discussion with a good friend.

    Before going any further, there are two places where one can get a good idea of the detailed history of the Tablet and also some of the themes. So I recommend to the reader to go to the "Crimson Academy" that has a page of videos about the Tablet, and also to the Chapter 5 of "Revelation of Baha’u’llah, Volume 2" by Adib Taherzadeh, which discusses this Tablet.

    The online links are:

    https://thecrimsonacademy.com/ta (Academy)

    https://d9263461.github.io/cl/Baha'i/Others/ROB/V2/p107-136Ch05.html (Rev.)

    The Tablet of Ahmad is perhaps the most popular prayer in the Baha’i Faith. My good friend Patrick and I used to say it together every morning to start our day. I have kept this habit after Patrick passed away, and I find that if I forget to say the Tablet to start my day, then all sorts of things “go wrong” and my day is not “smooth”.

    I will have in the following some historical comments, an explanation of how the Baha’i concept of martyrdom is different than in previous Religions, and the explanation to two passages in the Tablet, "Be as a flame of fire to My enemies…" and "... he who turneth away from this Beauty hath also turned away from the Messengers of the past..."

    A few historical comments

    Let me first mention that Ahmad was one of the guys at the Garden of Ridvan where Baha'u'llah declared His Mission openly, after having waited about 10 years since He received Revelation in the Siyah Chal [dark pit]. So Ahmad left us some details about that time period. Especially the last (12th) day when Baha'u'llah was about to leave and He ordered most of those present to go out and teach His Faith and not to come with Him on the exile from Baghdad to Constantinople (Istanbul).

    For example, Ahmad said that once they found out that they were not going to accompany Baha'u'llah, many fell to tears. One believer recited a poem by a famous Persian poet, Sa’di, "Let us shed tears as clouds pour in the spring. Even the stones wail as the lovers part." [It was pouring rain on that day.] Baha'u'llah replied that "The Poem was really created for this day."

    Ahmad also said that Baha'u'llah explained to them that some of the people that were going with Him were people He truly needed and relied on, such as Abdu'l-Baha for example. And that other people were some that He wanted to "keep an eye on" so they don't cause too much mischief. [That referred to MIrza Yahyah of course, and his band of losers.]

    It is very interesting to note that although some guys that were at the Garden of Ridvan wrote about the time period, such as our early historian Nabil and Ahmad, not a single one of those souls ever described the manner in which Baha'u'llah Revealed Himself. No one told us how Baha'u'llah did it, and what He said when He did it, and what different reactions people had.

    The best explanation for this mystery is that the guys that were there wrote about it as if they were living in a Fairy Tale. And mentally and spiritually speaking, they were "somewhere else"..

    Shoghi Effendi has said that the Baha'i historians of the future will have great difficulty in penetrating this mystery - that we have no idea how exactly Baha'u'llah Declared.

    Ahmad and Nabil being present at the garden of Ridvan is a fact actually very strongly related to both their stories. They left, as ordered, but both of them could not stay away from Baha'u'llah, and chose to go back and find their Lord.

    In the case of Nabil, he caught up with the Caravan of Baha'u'llah that was travelling on foot through the whole Middle East to get from Baghdad to Constantinople. Nabil caught up with them about halfway through the journey.

    In the case of Ahmad, it gets more interesting. Ahmad went out to teach all the Babis he could find in the Persian and Ottoman empires, and even got into a number of physical fights in the process of telling them that Baha'u'llah was the One that the Bab had announced, "Him Whom God Shall Make Manifest".

    But after a while, Ahmad realized that he could not stay away from his Lord Baha'u'llah. So Ahmad headed for Constantinople to find Baha'u'llah over there.

    When he got to Constantinople, he found out that Baha'u'llah had already been exiled to Adrianople. To his (Ahmad) surprise, he found that Baha'u'llah had left a Tablet for him - which meant that Baha'u'llah knew he was going to come. This Tablet is the famous "Tablet of Ahmad" - the most popular prayer in the Baha'i Faith.

    Well Ahmad read his Tablet. And after reading it, he realized that he had to give up his own will and submit himself to God's will. So Ahmad then realized that he had to give up on pursuing Baha'u'llah's location, and instead that he had to go back to teaching the Faith...

    It is said that Ahmad carried the original Tablet in his hand, and that he was known to say it at least five times a day. He is also known to have lived a very long life, at least over a hundred years old. According to one account, he lived to be 107. According to another one, Ahmad was 127 when he passed away!

    The Baha’i concept of martyrdom

    Member of the House of Justice, Glenford Mitchell, spoke about this topic in a talk in Haifa, Israel, in 1995. Mr. Mitchell explained clearly that in the Baha'i Faith, there is a huge difference between martyrdom and suicide. They are not the same thing.

    Nowadays, for example, extremists Muslims who strap bombs to their chests and go blow up a place are *not* martyrs - as they are led to believe. That is a suicide. And even worse, it is also homicide.

    Basically, if you go looking for it, it is a suicide. If you did not go looking for it, it is martyrdom, meaning, if someone puts you to the test to choose between denying your Faith or losing your life - and you refuse to deny your Faith - *that* is martyrdom.

    A couple of guys went to an island that was hostile to Baha'is and they got killed, and the Universal House of Justice stated that they were *not* martyrs because they went looking for it and knew what they were getting into.

    Basically, if you do not go looking for death, but you are at the right place, at the right time, for the ultimate sacrifice, then you are a martyr.

    Along those lines it is interesting to note that the Universal House of Justice does *not* want Baha'is from around the world to deliberately go to Iran, where the Faith has been persecuted 175 years. That would be seeking out death, and would be suicide. The House of Justice also adds, on this topic, "The Last thing that the Universal House of Justice wants would be to lose the best elements of the Baha'i Faith throughout the world..."

    Now I have to mention some of the history of the Baha'i Faith with regards to martyrdom. This explanation is based on the book series "Revelation of Baha'u'llah" by Adib Taherzadeh, a Baha'i engineer and Counsellor who eventually got elected to the House of Justice - where he served 12 years until he died.

    In the early days of the Faith, we needed martyrs - people who were not afraid to lose their lives, as a result of spreading the Faith. Remember that in the first 6 years alone, we lost 10,000 believers that were massacred, including elders, children, and even babies.

    Also, as a former Police Officer, I want to point out that were many crimes against the Baha'i Community in those days - not just stealing of Baha'i property and businesses - but also major crimes such as a large amount of rapes of Baha'i women, which, to my surprise, most Baha'i historians never mention at all.

    Well when the Faith started and for a number of decades, Baha'u'llah had given to the martyrs the highest possible spiritual rank that a believer could achieve - not denying one's Faith when face to face with death as a result.

    It was therefore a number of years before the Faith had grown to a point where Baha'u'llah indicated that was no longer the best thing. He changed this "top: injunction and said that the highest rank a believer could achieve was to spend his/her life dedicated to teaching His Faith.

    So when you read the Writings of Baha'u'llah, you will see both of those injunctions. Just be aware that the early years of the Faith needed souls that were not afraid to be put to death. But that later, what the Faith needed was those dedicated to teaching it.

    Many decades later, Shoghi Effendi [The Guardian] coined the phrase "Living Martyr" and explained that a Baha'i who sacrifices every day of his life for the Faith is a type of martyr... a "living martyr"... And that is what is really needed in this day and age.

    Explanation of the phrase "Be as a flame of fire to my enemies..."

    In a talk given in Haifa, Israel, 1995, Adib Taherzadeh explained that in order to understand this verse, he looked up every single instance of Baha'u'llah referring to being "as a flame". He explains that not a single time did Baha'u'llah mean any kind of aggressive behavior towards enemies or any kind of offence [as in the opposite of defense].

    The "Flame of Fire" refers to being so on fire with the way that you defend the Faith from attacks that it [the flame] melts away any attack with its warmth. So basically, it refers to a defensive tactic.

    So the phrase was never meant as any physical attack on enemies of the Faith, which would be contrary to Baha'i teachings.

    Explanation of the phrase "... he who turneth away from this Beauty hath also turned away from the Messengers of the past..."

    To understand this concept, I would like to relate a funny incident where an Israel Investigator accused me of lying about being a Baha'i because the answer I gave to a question that he asked was not the answer that he was used to hearing. This gave way to a 30 minute conversation, at the end of which he realized that I was, indeed, a Baha'i, but that I was thinking about that question in a total different way than most of the Baha'i population.

    My friend Adam Austin and I went to Israel in December 1999. I was granted permission to visit Haifa for 3 nights. Our intention was to visit the rest of Israel for a couple of weeks. However, Adam changed his mind and decided we should try to make it back to Louisiana for Christmas with his family.

    I was not yet a Policeman, but I was a Criminal Justice scholar (student) and I told Adam, "The cops are going to split us and see if our stories match." Indeed, two guys of different religions travelling together through Israel who cancel their stay by 2 weeks cannot be more suspicious of a situation to Israel Police. They had to make sure we were not terrorists who dropped a bomb somewhere and were "hauling ass" out of there. In other words, who plans a trip to the Holy Land to cancel the trip once there? Nobody!

    Well, sure enough, they backtracked everywhere we were. And they sent me a Detective or Investigator that was a "specialist in the Baha'i Faith."

    First, the guy confronted me, "You said you and Adam travelled together. But on the afternoon of your third day, you were in the Shrine of the Bab and Adam was visiting the Cave of Elijah." To this I replied, "I said we were travelling together. But I never said we were married!." The Investigator immediately dropped that line of questioning, since Middle Eastern people are very sensitive to the whole homosexual issues.

    Well, this "specialist of the Baha'i Faith" proceeded to question me about the Baha'i Faith. He eventually asked me, "How does the Baha'i Faith view different Religions?" I gave him my personal answer, "There is only ONE religion and it's called the Religion of God. And that Religion evolves from age to age."

    Immediately, the Detective accused me of not being a Baha'i and accused me of lying about me. He really believed that he had me cornered, as an imposter. He told me that the correct Baha'i answer to his question was that "Different Religions have a spiritual side and a social side. The spiritual side is the same, as the Founders of different religions all taught to love God, love your wife, love your neighbor, do good, etc. What changes are the Laws of each Religion, which are adapted to the "times" that the Religion appears."

    Well... LOL... I told him that "Every person in the Baha'i Faith is free to see these things however he or she likes", and I then proceeded, in the span of 30 minutes, to show him how deep I felt my answer was and that in particular, the two answers were NOT contrary to each other at all...

    If you look at it from the point of view of humanity being a child that is attending different Grades of school, one after another, then it is clear that the Founders of the world Religions were all working hand-in-hand towards a common education.

    There is also a belief in the Baha'i Faith that the "Hosts of Heaven" are with us, being that we are the current Religion for the problems of today, and that those forces assist the progress of the Baha'i Faith and remove obstacles that are "in the way" of this progress. Whether this is true or not, it is something that most active Baha'is believe.

    One cannot fail to notice that these so-called "different" Religions came many years apart - on average 1000 years; but as early as 600 years and as late as 2100 years. So basically, they did not appear at the same time. They each had a Divine Mandate to illumine the world and make it progress and bring people back to the light.

    Also, from the Baha'i point of view, every Religion has predicted the Religion(s) to follow its coming. And when the new Religion appears, about 1000 years later, it is as a "final exam" for the students about to enter a new year of school.

    So from that point of view, the new Religion is not different from the previous one, but it is a new phase in the education of the world.

    Also, from that point of view, any student who does not wish to move forward to his next “grade of education” is failing everything that his teacher has taught him.

    Even a student who says that he loves his Teacher so much that he won’t advance to another Grade is failing everything that his Teacher prepared him for.

    You see very clearly in the evolution of the Teachings through the ages that those Religions are all part of the same education. For example, we went from the unity of man and his soul, to the unity of the family, to the unity of the tribe, to the unity of cities, to the unity of a Nation, and now we are in the phase of the unity of the world. Maybe one day in about 1000 years, we will have the unity with alien races on other planets.

    In a nutshell, these Religions all being each a phase of the eternal "Religion of God" is not in any way contrary to the fact that each have a common spiritual side and yet different Teachings meant for the age in which they appear.

    And finally, denying this "Beauty" is denying "the Messengers of the past" for failing to follow what they prepared you for. Especially if it is the same "Religion of God", you are denying it by denying the current phase it is in.

      Ryan Kazemi
